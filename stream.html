2Install OBS Studio

The free app that sends the picture to Twitch, where you can see and control everything.

  1. Download OBS Studio for Windows
  2. Open the file you downloaded and press Next until it’s finished.
  3. Open OBS Studio. If it asks whether to run the Auto-Configuration Wizard, press No: the next two steps set everything by hand, for the sharpest picture.

3Connect OBS to Twitch

OBS’s Settings has every option in one window. The numbers match the picture.

  1. In OBS, click Settings (bottom right), then Stream in the list on the left.
  2. Service: Twitch.
  3. Press Connect Account (recommended) and log in to Twitch in the box that opens.
  4. Press Apply. Keep the Settings window open for the next step.
  1. In OBS, click Settings (bottom right), then Stream in the list on the left.
  2. Service: pick Kick if it’s in the list, otherwise Custom…
  3. On Kick, open your creator dashboard, then Settings, Stream Key. Copy the Stream URL into OBS’s Server box and the Stream Key into OBS’s Stream Key box.
  4. Press Apply. Keep the Settings window open for the next step.

5Put Phosphor Loop in OBS

This file tells OBS everything: the picture, its size, your computer’s sound (your microphone starts muted).

  1. In OBS’s top menu, click Scene Collection, then Import.
  2. In the box that opens, click … and pick Phosphor Loop for OBS (it’s in your Downloads folder).
  3. Make sure the little box at the start of the line is ticked, then press Import, then Close.
  4. Click Scene Collection again and pick Phosphor Loop. The picture appears in OBS.

Test version only, the first time: this test copy comes through a free service that first shows a page saying One more step, and OBS shows it too. Click past it once and it won’t ask again:

  1. In OBS, right-click the picture and choose Interact.
  2. In the window that opens, click Open the page, then close that window.
