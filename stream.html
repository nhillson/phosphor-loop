PHOSPHOR LOOP ON TWITCH OR KICK

Phosphor Loop streams to Twitch or Kick hidden in the background: no OBS, no windows. You get one icon on your desktop. Double-click it to go live; double-click it again to stop.

1Download it

One small file for Windows. If Chrome asks whether to keep it, choose Keep.

2Open it

Double-click the file you downloaded. Windows doesn’t know it yet, so it warns you once:

  1. Click More info.
  2. Click Run anyway.

That puts a Phosphor Loop on Twitch icon on your desktop and starts it.

3Tell it where you stream (first time only)

A small box asks a few things. They stay on your computer; the stream key is stored encrypted.

  1. Under Where do you stream?, pick Twitch or Kick.
  2. Twitch: paste your stream key. To get it, click the blue Where is it? link in the box: Twitch opens your stream settings. Press Copy next to Primary Stream key, then click in the box and press Ctrl+V.
  3. Kick: the box also asks for your Stream URL. Click the blue Where are they? link: on Kick, open Settings, then Stream Key, and copy the Stream URL and the Stream Key into their boxes.
  4. Press Go live.

The first start downloads Phosphor Loop and ffmpeg (the part that sends video to Twitch or Kick), so it takes a few minutes. After that it starts in about half a minute.

4You’re live

A small purple icon appears by the clock. Hover over it to see how it’s doing; right-click it for the menu.

  1. To stop: double-click the desktop icon again (or right-click the tray icon, Stop streaming).
  2. Next time: just double-click the desktop icon.
  3. While live: right-click the tray icon, Open the Go live page, to skip to a new look and change settings.

Good to know

  1. It streams everything your computer plays, including notification sounds. Mute those (or turn on Do not disturb) while you’re live.
  2. Twitch and Kick mute or remove streams with copyrighted songs (most of what’s on Spotify), so play music you’re allowed to stream.
  3. Your computer stays awake while it streams. Keep it plugged in.
