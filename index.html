      QUICK GUIDE · ABOUT 3 MINUTES

      How to play Phosphor Loop

      Video feedback is what happens when a camera films the screen it’s plugged into. Every frame goes back in a little zoomed, turned and tinted, so anything on screen leaves a trail of echoes. Phosphor Loop runs that loop sixty times a second, and you steer it.

      INPUTA pattern, picture, video, camera, screen or phone
      →
      LOOPZoom, spin, warp, fade and tint the last frame
      →
      SCREENWhat you see
      ↻ the screen is fed back into the loop sixty times a second

      Start hereYour first 30 seconds

      1. Pick an input. Under Inputs, choose what goes into the loop: a pattern, a picture, your camera, a 3D world or a fractal.
      2. Drag on the screen. You draw light straight into the loop and watch it echo.
      3. Move Zoom and Spin. They change the picture the most. Double-click a slider to put it back.
      4. Press Surprise me for something new, or Autopilot to let it run by itself.

      ReferenceWhat the controls do

      Autopilot

      Neutral
      Just loop A’s input filling the screen, with every effect off. A clean starting point: raise Trails, then Zoom and Spin.
      Surprise me
      Random settings. Press it a few times.
      Shared presets
      Looks picked out for everyone who uses Phosphor Loop. Tap one to load it. Ones marked AUTO start Autopilot with their own settings.
      My presets
      Save current look keeps every setting under a name, with a snapshot of the picture. Save while Autopilot is running and the preset keeps Autopilot’s settings and pins too: loading it starts Autopilot again from that look. Those presets are marked AUTO. Presets stay in this browser on this device. Save with the same name to update one.
      Autopilot
      Changes the settings slowly by itself, fading between the two loops every half minute or so. Every 1 to 3 new looks it also puts in a different nature picture, from the kind you chose in the gallery (a picture of your own stays put). It never lets the picture turn white: if too much of the screen gets close to white, it turns its own Trails, feeds and Grow down a notch on the loop that’s doing it, shows one loop instead of two, or moves on to the next look. Pinned settings and fader limits are always respected. Press it again to stop.
      3D worlds and Fractals under Autopilot
      Autopilot brings them in as a showcase now and then, about 1 new look in 15. Fractals also come up as an ordinary input, as often as Wave or Shape, with effects and timing like any other look. As a showcase, so you can see them, a 3D world or fractal arrives clean, with no Trails, Zoom, Spin, color shift, Warp, drift or effects, shown on its own and staying about 1⅔ times as long as other looks. Once it has faded in, its effects build up slowly, peak in the middle of its stay, and ease back to clean before the next look fades in over it. Pinned settings stay put, and a setting with a fader limit starts from that limit.
      Check each look
      Under Autopilot settings, on unless you switch it off. Autopilot tries each new look out of sight on the hidden loop first and looks at it for a few seconds. One that’s turning white, nearly black, plain or frozen is thrown away and another tried, up to six in all, and it keeps the richest. The status line at the top of Autopilot settings says when it’s trying looks. Switch it off to see every look Autopilot makes, straight away.
      More like this and Not this
      The thumb up and thumb down in the small strip right under the picture, or the Y and X keys. Beside them, a countdown shows how many seconds this look has left before Autopilot’s next one. In full screen during a taste session, a small timer and More like this and Not this appear at the bottom middle of the picture whenever you move the mouse (or tap the picture), and fade away again after a few still seconds (switch on “Keep timer and rating buttons showing in full screen” under Taste profiles to keep them showing). Whichever button you’ve pressed for the look on screen lights up green or red, so you can see you’ve already rated it. It stays lit the whole time that look is on screen (even while you move sliders, or Autopilot tries out its next look out of sight) and goes back to normal the moment a new look comes in, ready for the next rating. They teach Autopilot your taste. A rating is for the whole picture on screen: both loops together, how they’re shown and fed into each other (whether one pushes, dyes or is the input of the other, whether they zoom and turn alike or apart), and how the picture looks. Autopilot then picks new looks, and how to mix them, by the picture they’d make together. While Autopilot runs, Not this also skips to the next look. Rate the same picture the other way and the new rating replaces the old one. Ratings go into the lit taste profile (or the taste session, while one is under way) and stay in this browser.
      Taste profiles
      Your ratings are kept in named taste profiles, and Autopilot follows the lit one (tap another to switch, or tap the lit one again so Autopilot follows no taste at all). Ratings you make outside a session go into the lit profile; with none lit, rating starts a taste session. On your own profiles, ✎ renames, ⤓ saves the profile as a small file you can send to someone, and × deletes it. Open a taste file… (or dropping the file on the page) adds a profile someone sent you. Profiles marked SHARED come from the admin; rating while one is lit starts your own copy of it.
      Taste session
      Start taste session, under Taste profiles, makes a new profile from scratch. Autopilot brings looks, you rate them with More like this and Not this, and Autopilot learns from them as you go. A green bar under the picture counts your ratings; press Finish and name it to keep the profile, or Cancel (twice, once you’ve rated) to throw it away.
      Train again
      With one of your profiles lit, Train “…” again starts another session for it: Autopilot follows the profile as it would live, you rate what it brings, and Finish and add them puts the new ratings into the profile. Made for one profile per song, trained over and over until you know what each song will bring.
      What it learns
      Every setting of both loops (inputs, effects and how strong, mirror, zoom, spin, trails, color, warp, Input size, Blend, softness, Wave and Shape patterns, Grow’s size, Time warp’s direction, Only movement, edges, drift, brightness, the Spectrograph’s style), which nature picture is showing, and how the screen looks. And not just which ones you like, but which go together: each loop’s settings tied to its input (“Shape with Melt, zoomed in”), the effects that come together, whole looks, and whole pairs of looks with how they’re shown and fed into each other (“that Shape next to Wave with a 6-sided mirror, Wave feeding into Shape”). At Stick to what I like, Autopilot mostly brings back pictures you rated up, changed only a little, so you know roughly what will be on screen; How wild sets how much they change.
      Follow my taste
      Under Taste profiles. From Surprise me (Autopilot pays your taste no attention) to Stick to what I like; in the middle it leans your way but still tries new things. It starts working after a few ratings and gets good after about 15. Learn from hints also counts a skip in a new look’s first few seconds (a little against it) and a pinned setting (for that setting).
      Next look
      Beside Autopilot under the picture, under Autopilot settings, or the L key. Skips straight to Autopilot’s next new look, cutting in quickly, and starts Autopilot if it’s off. With Check each look on, a look that’s ready goes on at once; otherwise it takes two or three seconds to find one that passes. The wait for the look after starts over.
      Pins
      While Autopilot runs, a small pin shows beside each setting. Tap it (or just move that slider, with Pin it, keep going chosen) and the setting stays where it is on the loop you’re changing (the A or B tab) while Autopilot keeps changing the rest. Right-click a pin (or press and hold it on a touchscreen) to hold that setting on both loops at once, the same on each; the pin then says AB. Tap a lit pin to let it go on this loop, or right-click an AB pin to let it go on both. Under Autopilot settings, When you move a control while it runs offers two other ways: Play along (your move takes effect, nothing gets pinned, and Autopilot keeps going and changes it again with its next look) or Stop Autopilot.
      Autopilot settings
      How often a new look comes, how slowly it fades in, how wild it gets, and how often it shows both loops at once. How wild also sets how much each new look changes: at Calm only a few settings and inputs change, in the middle about 3 in 4 of them get something new (every effect is on in about half the looks), and at Wild nearly all of them change and most effects are on at once. Check each look tries looks out of sight and skips the poor ones. Change on the beat makes each new look cut in on a beat of the music. Under Inputs Autopilot can use, switch off any input you don’t want it to bring in, or right-click one (press and hold on a touchscreen) to choose how often it comes in: Off, Rarely, Less, Normal, More or Lots. The little pie beside each name shows its setting, and the menu says roughly what share of Autopilot’s picks it gets. For 3D worlds and Fractals the menu also lists every world and fractal, each with its own switch and setting. Switching off Picture also stops it changing the picture. Double-click a slider there to put it back.
      Fader limits
      The button under Autopilot settings. Each slider in it has two handles: Autopilot only picks values between them, on both loops (keep Trails between 40 and 90, say). It never moves the handles itself. Mirror is in there too, with its five stops (Off, 2, 4, 6, 8): its handles snap to them, and the stops they sit on count, so lowest 2 and highest 6 means Autopilot uses 2, 4 or 6. Sliders you move by hand and pinned ones can still go anywhere.

      Loops

      Tooltips and the mouse wheel
      Rest the mouse on a control’s name for a little over a second and a description of it pops up. Hold Shift and roll the mouse wheel over any fader to move it, 2 a notch on a 0 to 100 fader. Without Shift the wheel just scrolls the controls.
      Loops that react
      At the top of React to sound, and still showing when that section is folded: one switch per loop. A loop switched off there keeps its own pace whatever the music does, while the loops switched on zoom, spin, change color and so on with it. The hits that shake or flash the whole picture are as strong as the share of the picture that comes from loops that react, so they stop altogether when every loop on screen is switched off. Presets remember these switches.
      How many loops and Show
      Two loops, A and B, to start with; pick 3 loops or 4 loops under Loops to add loop C, then D. Each loop has its own Show fader: 100 shows it fully, 0 hides it, and loops that are showing are laid over each other. Loops C and D work just like A and B: C and D feed, push and dye each other the way A and B do (with three loops, C takes loop A as its other loop). Autopilot steers loops A and B; loops C and D keep whatever you give them, while Auto-drift keeps them moving. With Spread out controls on, each loop gets a column of its own, side by side (two loops share a column when the screen is too narrow).
      Feed A into B, Feed B into A
      Pour one loop’s picture into the other loop’s trails. Use one for a one-way flow, or both and the loops trade shapes. The loop being fed has to be on screen and have some Trails; raising a feed brings that loop on screen, and if its Trails are at 0 a note offers to fix it.
      Loop A and Loop B tabs
      Pick which loop the controls below change. A red light means that loop is on screen.

      Inputs

      Wave, Shape, Bars, Sparks, Ink, Patchwork, 3D world, Fractals
      Built-in patterns that need no setup. Ink lands drops of color about twice a second; pair it with Flow. Patchwork is a hand-inked page of small ruled cells like a sketchbook: marker bars, triangles, bowties, striped circles, eyes, mountains and half moons, plus line tiles (straights, curls, square curls, weaves, crossroads, rings) whose lines run on into each other as paths, loops and mazes. Every few seconds a ripple runs across the page and tiles turn, making pinwheels, diamonds, zigzags and new paths. Every 15 seconds or so the rule for how they turn (random, diagonal waves, spinning checks, rings, a four-way mirror) and the mix of tiles change: Sketchbook (most often), Quilt, Maze, Cloth, Circuit or Everything. A smaller Input size makes it a page in the middle. 3D world opens a picker of ten 3D worlds to fly through, circle round, drift past or, in Mount Ribbit, walk on (W A S D walk, Shift runs, J jumps). Fractals opens a picker of six 3D fractals (Menger sponge, Mandelbulb, Mandelbox, Sierpinski, Julia and Kaleidoscope) with a camera that wanders on its own: it rolls and turns, sweeps round the outside, dives deep into the endless detail and climbs back out, and each loop takes its own route. Input size widens or narrows the view, and with React to sound on, the flight surges on the kick and the lights flare on the beat.
      Spectrograph
      A picture of the sound itself: low sounds at the bottom, high sounds at the top, and the louder, the brighter. Pick Computer sound or Microphone right under it (it uses the same listening as React to sound). Scrolling slides to the left like a heart monitor; Round ripples out from the middle and makes great tunnels with Zoom. It sets its own brightness, so quiet rooms and loud music both show up. To see only the spectrograph without the picture pumping to the beat, set Amount under React to sound to 0.
      Text
      Your own words: a name, a title, the venue. Type them in the Your text box that opens under the inputs and they change as you type. Bold, Outline, Classic and Typewriter change the lettering. Long text wraps onto two or three lines by itself. Presets keep the words they were saved with.
      Picture
      Nature pictures… opens a gallery of about 500 photos: sea life, birds, bugs, frogs, flowers, mushrooms and more. Tap one to feed it in. ◀ ▶ and Shuffle flip through the kind you chose without opening the gallery. Your own picture… loads a file of yours.
      Video
      Your own file. Video gets a tape deck: scrub, skip 5 seconds, change speed, and repeat a section.
      Camera, Screen, Phone
      Live pictures. Screen shares this tab so the screen films itself. Phone shows a QR code to scan.
      Loop A’s or Loop B’s picture
      Feed the other loop straight in as an input. At Input size 50 it lines up with the screen.
      Second input and Blend
      Mix two inputs into the same loop.
      Input size
      How big the input sits on screen, up to filling it.
      Only movement
      For live inputs and video: only what moves gets in.
      Adjust the picture

      Effects

      Zoom
      In makes a tunnel. Out shrinks the picture into itself.
      Spin
      Twists every echo. Small amounts make spirals.
      Trails
      How long echoes last. High is dreamy, low is snappy, and None turns the feedback off.
      Color shift
      Moves each echo a little further round the rainbow.
      Warp
      Wobbles the echoes like water.
      Flow
      Slow currents curl the echoes into threads and swirls, like ink in water. Works best with Trails high.
      Melt
      The picture pushes itself around. How bright each spot is decides which way it slides, and edges curl along themselves, so the echoes keep folding into each other like marbling. Low is a gentle drip; high is wild. Works best with Trails high.
      Pushed by the other loop
      Like Melt, but the other loop does the pushing. Its bright parts decide which way this loop slides and its outlines drag this loop’s trails along them, so the other loop’s shapes show up as currents and swirls. It works even while the other loop is hidden. Works best with Trails high.
      Dyed by the other loop
      The other loop soaks color into this one. Wherever it’s bright, this loop’s colors keep turning round the rainbow, so its shapes show up here as shifting rainbow bands. Grey and white parts pick up color too.
      Grow
      The picture grows patterns out of itself, the way stripes and spots form on animals: coral, zebra stripes, fingerprints, mazes. They start at outlines and spread through anything lit, and keep re-forming as the loop zooms and spins. Fine, Medium and Big set how far apart the stripes sit. Low is a fine texture, high takes over the whole picture. Keep Trails high.
      Mirror
      A kaleidoscope with 2 to 8 sides.
      Split lines
      Every other line of the picture slides left and the lines between slide right, so echoes tear apart like a scrambled TV.
      Time warp
      Each part of the picture shows a different moment. With Top to bottom the top is right now and the bottom is up to about a second and a half ago; Side to side runs from left to right; From the middle has now in the middle and the past round the edges. Anything moving stretches into ribbons and waves, and turning patterns twist. The number shows how far back the far edge reaches. Wonderful with the camera or phone: wave your arms.

      Effects: feeds on itself

      Look-up
      Each spot copies from a spot its own color points to: how red it is says how far to the side, how green how far up or down, and how blue turns that direction round. The picture keeps rearranging itself by its own colors into folds and liquid-metal swirls. Dark parts stay put. More Color shift gives it more to work with.
      Chase
      Zoom and Spin normally turn around the middle. With Chase they turn around the brightest part of the picture, and as the tunnel pulls that light away, it heads for the next bright spot, so the middle of the tunnel keeps wandering. Needs some Zoom or Spin.
      Breathe
      Like React to sound, but the loop listens to itself. As the picture gets brighter it zooms out, as it gets darker it zooms in, and the more it moves the faster it spins. It settles into its own slow rhythm, and pulls back before it turns white.
      Color chase
      Red is pushed around by the green parts of the picture, green by the blue and blue by the red. Each color slides along the outlines of the one pushing it, so the three chase each other round and split into rainbow fringes and braids.
      Restless
      Only what's changing gets to stay. Each frame the picture is compared with the one before, and the parts that held still fade away, leaving a shimmer of moving outlines. Lovely with the camera.
      Fractal fold
      Instead of a plain zoom, every frame folds the picture through the formula behind the Mandelbrot set, so anything that comes in grows into fractal flowers, spirals and lace. The fractal slowly changes shape by itself; Zoom and Spin reshape it too. Low mixes a little of it into the usual tunnel.
      Crystal
      The picture cracks into ten pieces centred on its brightest spots. Each piece turns and zooms in its own way, like a broken mirror, and the cracks move as the light does.

      React to sound

      Computer sound
      Listens to whatever this computer is playing: Spotify, YouTube, games. Chrome asks what to share: on the Entire Screen tab pick any screen (with two monitors, Screen 1 or Screen 2 both give the whole computer’s sound) and switch on Also share system audio. For Spotify in a browser tab, pick that tab and switch on Also share tab audio.
      Microphone
      Listens to the room, so music from a speaker or a clap works. Listen to picks which input when there’s more than one.
      Loopback adapter
      Hears the computer through BlackHole, Soundflower, Stereo Mix or another installed loopback input, without screen sharing. Pick the adapter under Listen to. On a Mac, route your output to a multi-output device that includes BlackHole and your speakers or headphones. Allow “microphone” access when the browser asks; that permission also covers loopback adapters. Video sound set to Computer keeps this same sound in recordings.
      Stereo Mix (Windows)
      Hears the computer’s sound with no screen sharing at all: choose Microphone, then Stereo Mix under Listen to. If it isn’t listed, turn it on once in Windows Settings → System → Sound → More sound settings (Sound Control Panel on Windows 10) → Recording tab → right-click an empty spot → Show Disabled Devices → right-click Stereo Mix → Enable, then reload this page. Some computers don’t have it.
      BPM and the meter
      Low, Mid and High bars respond immediately. After about 12 seconds of music, the BPM readout and BEAT light follow a steady tempo grid. This pulse drives beat effects and Autopilot’s Change on the beat. Pulses stop during silence or when tempo tracking is unavailable. Run from localhost or HTTPS for BPM tracking.
      Amount and What the music moves
      How hard the picture reacts (the middle is already strong; Wild is very strong), and which settings follow the music: Zoom kick punches the picture in on the BPM pulse and bass, Input size swells on the bass, Spin follows the mids, Color jumps with the highs and beats, Warp jolts the picture on beats and Flash brightens on beats.
      Which browser
      Computer sound works in Chrome and Edge. Firefox and Safari don’t pass sound through screen sharing, so use Microphone there (Stereo Mix on Windows hears the computer itself).
      Record
      Video sound, next to Record, picks what goes into the video: the microphone, this computer’s sound, or both mixed together. Whatever React to sound is already hearing goes straight in, with nothing more to allow.

      Screen and buttons

      Screen shape
      Fill the space, or frame it as a 4:3 TV or 16:9.
      Picture sharpness
      How many lines the picture is drawn with; the number shows at the top right. Standard is the 576 lines Phosphor Loop has always used and is the lightest on the computer. High goes up to 1080, like HD video. Full draws as many lines as the screen (or the Output window) shows. Auto, the usual setting, starts where it last settled on this computer, sharpens while the picture stays smooth and steps back if it slows down. Effects keep their size at every setting; only the detail gets finer. Each computer or phone keeps its own choice.
      Freeze and Clear
      Hold the picture, or wipe it to black.
      Auto-drift
      Slowly moves the settings for you. Good for leaving it running.
      Full screen
      Show only the picture. To leave full screen, move the mouse (or tap the picture) and press Exit full screen at the top right, or press Esc.
      Output window
      Opens the picture in its own window for a projector or second screen. Drag it over and double-click it to fill that screen. The controls stay here, and you can draw on the projected picture too.
      Spread out controls
      Press it and the picture shrinks to a small preview at the top left, and the controls spread out in columns across the rest of the screen, so you can see many more of them at once. It works with or without the Output window. Press it again for the big picture. Phosphor Loop remembers your choice.
      Save picture and Record
      Save a still, or a video as long as you like: press Record again to stop and save it. The V key does the same, even in full screen. Before anything saves, a box asks for a name: type one, or press Enter to keep the usual name. Videos are large, up to about 75 MB a minute.
      Video sound
      Which sound goes into videos you record. Click it to step through Off, Mic, Computer and Both (mic and computer mixed). If React to sound is already hearing that sound, the video simply keeps it. If not, Record asks first: for Mic the browser asks to use the microphone (choose Allow); for Computer, Chrome’s sharing box opens (pick a screen on the Entire Screen tab, switch on Also share system audio, press Share). On the phone app it says Phone: choose Phone sound under React to sound first.

      • Fold away sections you’re not using by clicking their headings, or use Collapse all at the top of the controls.

      TroubleshootingIf something looks wrong

      You’ve lost track of what you changed
      Press Neutral to get back to just the input, then build up again.
      The screen went white
      Autopilot keeps it from turning white by itself. When you’re steering, lower Trails, Input size or Color shift a little, or turn down Feed A into B and Feed B into A. Press Clear to wipe it and start fresh.
      The screen is black
      Raise Trails, make Input size bigger, or drag on the screen. Check the input isn’t set to Nothing.
      Nothing is moving
      Check that Freeze is off, then move Zoom or Spin, or turn on Auto-drift.
      Camera, Screen or Phone won’t start
      Allow access when the browser asks. The phone link only works from the https:// web address, with both devices online.
      The output window didn’t open
      The browser blocked it. Click the blocked pop-up icon at the right of the address bar, choose to always allow pop-ups for this page, then press Output window again.
      The picture doesn’t react to music
      Check that the Low, Mid and High bars move. With Computer sound, share again and make sure Also share system audio is switched on under the screen you pick (or Also share tab audio for a browser tab). If that switch never appears, use Microphone with Stereo Mix instead. Then raise Amount.
      The Spectrograph is black
      It only draws what it hears. Press Computer sound or Microphone under it, allow it when the browser asks, and play something. If the Low, Mid and High bars under React to sound don’t move, it isn’t hearing anything yet; the tip above helps.
      It’s choppy
      Close other tabs, or choose 4:3 TV as the screen shape.
      It won’t open without internet
      It needs one visit from its web address with internet first. Open it once online, wait a few seconds, and from then on it opens offline.
