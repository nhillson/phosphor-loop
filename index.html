    Phosphor Loop

    QUICK GUIDE · ABOUT 3 MINUTES

    How to play Phosphor Loop

    Video feedback is what happens when a camera films the screen it’s plugged into. Every frame goes back in a little zoomed, turned and tinted, so anything on screen leaves a trail of echoes. Phosphor Loop runs that loop sixty times a second, and you steer it.

    INPUTA pattern, picture, video, camera, screen or phone
    →
    LOOPZoom, spin, warp, fade and tint the last frame
    →
    SCREENWhat you see
    ↻ the screen is fed back into the loop sixty times a second

    Start hereYour first 30 seconds

    1. Pick a channel. The buttons at the top of the controls are ready-made looks.
    2. Drag on the screen. You draw light straight into the loop and watch it echo.
    3. Move Zoom and Spin. They change the picture the most. Double-click a slider to put it back.
    4. Press Surprise me for something new, or Autopilot to let it run by itself.

    ReferenceWhat the controls do

    Channels

    Channels
    Ready-made looks. Twins and Weave use both loops. Ink drops colored ink into swirling water. Cubes to City are 3D worlds: floating cubes, a sunset valley, a coral cave, a black hole, northern lights, liquid metal, a fractal hall, a nebula and a wet city street at night drifting sideways. Coral grows zebra stripes and mazes out of a mandala, and Ribbons stretches a spinning pattern through time.
    Neutral
    Just loop A’s input filling the screen, with every effect off. A clean starting point: raise Trails, then Zoom and Spin.
    Surprise me
    Random settings. Press it a few times.
    Shared presets
    Looks picked out for everyone who uses Phosphor Loop. Tap one to load it. Ones marked AUTO start Autopilot with their own settings.
    My presets
    Save current look keeps every setting under a name, with a snapshot of the picture. Save while Autopilot is running and the preset keeps Autopilot’s settings and pins too: loading it starts Autopilot again from that look. Those presets are marked AUTO. Presets stay in this browser on this device. Save with the same name to update one.
    Autopilot
    Changes the settings slowly by itself, fading between the two loops every half minute or so. Every 1 to 3 new looks it also puts in a different nature picture, from the kind you chose in the gallery (a picture of your own stays put). It never lets the picture turn white: if too much of the screen gets close to white, it turns its own Trails, feeds and Grow down a notch on the loop that’s doing it, shows one loop instead of two, or moves on to the next look. Pinned settings and fader limits are always respected. Press it again to stop.
    Check each look
    Under Autopilot settings, on unless you switch it off. Autopilot tries each new look out of sight on the hidden loop first and looks at it for a few seconds. One that’s turning white, nearly black, plain or frozen is thrown away and another tried, up to six in all, and it keeps the richest. The status line at the top of Autopilot settings says when it’s trying looks. Switch it off to see every look Autopilot makes, straight away.
    Next look
    Beside Autopilot under the picture, under Autopilot settings, or the L key. Skips straight to Autopilot’s next new look, cutting in quickly, and starts Autopilot if it’s off. With Check each look on, a look that’s ready goes on at once; otherwise it takes two or three seconds to find one that passes. The wait for the look after starts over.
    Pins
    While Autopilot runs, a small pin shows beside each setting. Tap it, or just move that slider, and the setting stays where it is on the loop you’re changing (the A or B tab) while Autopilot keeps changing the rest. Right-click a pin (or press and hold it on a touchscreen) to hold that setting on both loops at once, the same on each; the pin then says AB. Tap a lit pin to let it go on this loop, or right-click an AB pin to let it go on both. Want touching a control to stop Autopilot instead? Choose Stop Autopilot under Autopilot settings.
    Autopilot settings
    How often a new look comes, how slowly it fades in, how wild it gets, and how often it shows both loops at once. Check each look tries looks out of sight and skips the poor ones. Change on the beat makes each new look cut in on a beat of the music. Under Inputs Autopilot can use, switch off any input you don’t want it to bring in. Switching off Picture also stops it changing the picture. Double-click a slider there to put it back.
    Fader limits
    The button under Autopilot settings. Each slider in it has two handles: Autopilot only picks values between them, on both loops (keep Trails between 40 and 90, say). It never moves the handles itself. Sliders you move by hand and pinned ones can still go anywhere.

    Mix the two loops

    What you see
    Show loop A, loop B, or both laid over each other.
    Feed A into B, Feed B into A
    Pour one loop’s picture into the other loop’s trails. Use one for a one-way flow, or both and the loops trade shapes. The loop being fed has to be on screen and have some Trails; raising a feed brings that loop on screen, and if its Trails are at 0 a note offers to fix it.
    Loop A and Loop B tabs
    Pick which loop the controls below change. A red light means that loop is on screen.

    What feeds the loop

    Wave, Shape, Bars, Sparks, Ink, 3D world
    Built-in patterns that need no setup. Ink lands drops of color about twice a second; pair it with Flow. 3D world opens a picker of nine 3D worlds to fly through, circle round or drift past. Input size widens or narrows the view, and with React to sound on, the flight surges on the kick and the lights flare on the beat.
    Spectrograph
    A picture of the sound itself: low sounds at the bottom, high sounds at the top, and the louder, the brighter. Pick Computer sound or Microphone right under it (it uses the same listening as React to sound). Scrolling slides to the left like a heart monitor; Round ripples out from the middle and makes great tunnels with Zoom. It sets its own brightness, so quiet rooms and loud music both show up. To see only the spectrograph without the picture pumping to the beat, set Amount under React to sound to 0.
    Text
    Your own words: a name, a title, the venue. Type them in the Your text box that opens under the inputs and they change as you type. Bold, Outline, Classic and Typewriter change the lettering. Long text wraps onto two or three lines by itself. Presets keep the words they were saved with.
    Picture
    Nature pictures… opens a gallery of about 500 photos: sea life, birds, bugs, frogs, flowers, mushrooms and more. Tap one to feed it in. ◀ ▶ and Shuffle flip through the kind you chose without opening the gallery (they work from a MIDI controller too). Your own picture… loads a file of yours.
    Video
    Your own file. Video gets a tape deck: scrub, skip 5 seconds, change speed, and repeat a section.
    Camera, Screen, Phone
    Live pictures. Screen shares this tab so the screen films itself. Phone shows a QR code to scan.
    Loop A’s or Loop B’s picture
    Feed the other loop straight in as an input. At Input size 50 it lines up with the screen.
    Second input and Blend
    Mix two inputs into the same loop.
    Input size
    How big the input sits on screen, up to filling it.
    Only movement
    For live inputs and video: only what moves gets in.
    Adjust the picture
    Under each picture, video or live input: Tracking (worn-tape wobble), Brightness, Contrast, Color, Cut out dark parts, and Hold this frame.

    Shape the loop

    Zoom
    In makes a tunnel. Out shrinks the picture into itself.
    Spin
    Twists every echo. Small amounts make spirals.
    Trails
    How long echoes last. High is dreamy, low is snappy, and None turns the feedback off.
    Color shift
    Moves each echo a little further round the rainbow.
    Warp
    Wobbles the echoes like water.
    Flow
    Slow currents curl the echoes into threads and swirls, like ink in water. Works best with Trails high.
    Melt
    The picture pushes itself around. How bright each spot is decides which way it slides, and edges curl along themselves, so the echoes keep folding into each other like marbling. Low is a gentle drip; high is wild. Works best with Trails high.
    Pushed by the other loop
    Like Melt, but the other loop does the pushing. Its bright parts decide which way this loop slides and its outlines drag this loop’s trails along them, so the other loop’s shapes show up as currents and swirls. It works even while the other loop is hidden. Works best with Trails high.
    Dyed by the other loop
    The other loop soaks color into this one. Wherever it’s bright, this loop’s colors keep turning round the rainbow, so its shapes show up here as shifting rainbow bands. Grey and white parts pick up color too.
    Grow
    The picture grows patterns out of itself, the way stripes and spots form on animals: coral, zebra stripes, fingerprints, mazes. They start at outlines and spread through anything lit, and keep re-forming as the loop zooms and spins. Fine, Medium and Big set how far apart the stripes sit. Low is a fine texture, high takes over the whole picture. Keep Trails high.
    Mirror
    A kaleidoscope with 2 to 8 sides.
    Split lines
    Every other line of the picture slides left and the lines between slide right, so echoes tear apart like a scrambled TV.
    Time warp
    Each part of the picture shows a different moment. With Top to bottom the top is right now and the bottom is up to about a second and a half ago; Side to side runs from left to right; From the middle has now in the middle and the past round the edges. Anything moving stretches into ribbons and waves, and turning patterns twist. The number shows how far back the far edge reaches. Wonderful with the camera or phone: wave your arms.

    Feeds on itself

    Look-up
    Each spot copies from a spot its own color points to: how red it is says how far to the side, how green how far up or down, and how blue turns that direction round. The picture keeps rearranging itself by its own colors into folds and liquid-metal swirls. Dark parts stay put. More Color shift gives it more to work with.
    Chase
    Zoom and Spin normally turn around the middle. With Chase they turn around the brightest part of the picture, and as the tunnel pulls that light away, it heads for the next bright spot, so the middle of the tunnel keeps wandering. Needs some Zoom or Spin.
    Breathe
    Like React to sound, but the loop listens to itself. As the picture gets brighter it zooms out, as it gets darker it zooms in, and the more it moves the faster it spins. It settles into its own slow rhythm, and pulls back before it turns white.
    Color chase
    Red is pushed around by the green parts of the picture, green by the blue and blue by the red. Each color slides along the outlines of the one pushing it, so the three chase each other round and split into rainbow fringes and braids.
    Restless
    Only what's changing gets to stay. Each frame the picture is compared with the one before, and the parts that held still fade away, leaving a shimmer of moving outlines. Lovely with the camera.
    Fractal fold
    Instead of a plain zoom, every frame folds the picture through the formula behind the Mandelbrot set, so anything that comes in grows into fractal flowers, spirals and lace. The fractal slowly changes shape by itself; Zoom and Spin reshape it too. Low mixes a little of it into the usual tunnel.
    Crystal
    The picture cracks into ten pieces centred on its brightest spots. Each piece turns and zooms in its own way, like a broken mirror, and the cracks move as the light does.

    React to sound

    Computer sound
    Listens to whatever this computer is playing: Spotify, YouTube, games. Chrome asks what to share: on the Entire Screen tab pick any screen (with two monitors, Screen 1 or Screen 2 both give the whole computer’s sound) and switch on Also share system audio. For Spotify in a browser tab, pick that tab and switch on Also share tab audio.
    Microphone
    Listens to the room, so music from a speaker or a clap works. Listen to picks which input when there’s more than one.
    Stereo Mix (Windows)
    Hears the computer’s sound with no screen sharing at all: choose Microphone, then Stereo Mix under Listen to. If it isn’t listed, turn it on once in Windows Settings → System → Sound → More sound settings (Sound Control Panel on Windows 10) → Recording tab → right-click an empty spot → Show Disabled Devices → right-click Stereo Mix → Enable, then reload this page. Some computers don’t have it.
    The meter
    Low, Mid and High bars and a BEAT light show what it hears. It adjusts itself to the volume.
    Amount and What the music moves
    How hard the picture reacts (the middle is already strong; Wild is very strong), and which settings follow the music: Zoom kick punches the picture in on every kick, Input size swells on the bass, Spin follows the mids, Color jumps with the highs and beats, Warp jolts the picture on beats and Flash brightens on beats.
    Which browser
    Computer sound works in Chrome and Edge. Firefox and Safari don’t pass sound through screen sharing, so use Microphone there (Stereo Mix on Windows hears the computer itself).
    Record
    Keeps the sound in the video while React to sound is on. With Include mic lit, the microphone goes in too, mixed with the music.

    Screen and buttons

    Screen shape
    Fill the space, or frame it as a 4:3 TV or 16:9.
    Freeze and Clear
    Hold the picture, or wipe it to black.
    Auto-drift
    Slowly moves the settings for you. Good for leaving it running.
    Full screen
    Show only the picture. To leave full screen, move the mouse (or tap the picture) and press Exit full screen at the top right, or press Esc.
    Output window
    Opens the picture in its own window for a projector or second screen. Drag it over and double-click it to fill that screen. The controls stay here, and you can draw on the projected picture too.
    Save picture and Record
    Save a still, or a video as long as you like: press Record again to stop and save it. Videos are large, up to about 75 MB a minute.
    Include mic
    When its light is on, videos you record include the microphone, so you can talk over the picture or catch the room. The first time, the browser asks to use the microphone: choose Allow. Press it again to turn the mic off.

    Show setup

    Show mode
    For a computer that plays at gigs. Each time Phosphor Loop starts it picks up the last look and Autopilot, reconnects the camera and microphone, opens the output window when a second screen is plugged in, and keeps the screen awake. Tap Fill my other screen in the output window to put the picture on the projector.
    Without internet
    After one visit from its web address, the browser keeps a copy, so Phosphor Loop opens offline. Only the phone link still needs internet. Nature pictures you’ve used are kept too; press Save all nature pictures to keep every one.
    MIDI controller
    Press Use a MIDI controller and allow it. Then press Learn, touch a slider or button, and move a fader, turn a knob or press a button on the controller. Repeat for each control. Mappings stay in this browser, like presets.
    Pickup
    A fader only takes over once it reaches the slider’s current position, so loading a channel or preset never makes the picture jump. Endless knobs work straight away.

    Hands onThings you can do with the screen

    • Draw by dragging on the screen with a mouse or finger. What you draw goes into whichever loops are showing, and Mirror copies every stroke.
    • Drop a file, a picture or a video, onto the screen to feed it in.
    • Double-click any slider to put it back where the channel had it.
    • Fold away sections you’re not using by clicking their headings, or use Collapse all at the top of the controls.

    TroubleshootingIf something looks wrong

    You’ve lost track of what you changed
    Press Neutral to get back to just the input, then build up again.
    The screen went white
    Autopilot keeps it from turning white by itself. When you’re steering, lower Trails, Input size or Color shift a little, or turn down Feed A into B and Feed B into A. Press Clear to wipe it and start fresh.
    The screen is black
    Raise Trails, make Input size bigger, or drag on the screen. Check the input isn’t set to Nothing.
    Nothing is moving
    Check that Freeze is off, then move Zoom or Spin, or turn on Auto-drift.
    Camera, Screen or Phone won’t start
    Allow access when the browser asks. The phone link only works from the https:// web address, with both devices online.
    The output window didn’t open
    The browser blocked it. Click the blocked pop-up icon at the right of the address bar, choose to always allow pop-ups for this page, then press Output window again.
    The picture doesn’t react to music
    Check that the Low, Mid and High bars move. With Computer sound, share again and make sure Also share system audio is switched on under the screen you pick (or Also share tab audio for a browser tab). If that switch never appears, use Microphone with Stereo Mix instead. Then raise Amount.
    The Spectrograph is black
    It only draws what it hears. Press Computer sound or Microphone under it, allow it when the browser asks, and play something. If the Low, Mid and High bars under React to sound don’t move, it isn’t hearing anything yet; the tip above helps.
    It’s choppy
    Close other tabs, or choose 4:3 TV as the screen shape.
    A fader on the MIDI controller does nothing
    It’s waiting to pick up: move it past the slider’s current position (a note under the screen says where). If nothing reacts at all, check the controller is listed under Show setup.
    It won’t open without internet
    It needs one visit from its web address with internet first. Open it once online, wait until Show setup says it’s saved, and from then on it opens offline.
